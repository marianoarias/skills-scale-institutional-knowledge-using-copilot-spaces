# OctoAcme Project Management Processes

Welcome to the OctoAcme Project Management Documentation. This collection provides standardized guidance for running successful projects across OctoAcme. The goal of these docs is to help teams move from idea to delivery with a consistent process, clear ownership, and measurable outcomes.

OctoAcme follows a structured project lifecycle that moves from initiation through planning, execution, release, and retrospective. The process begins with a Project One-pager that validates the business need, confirms stakeholder alignment, and defines success metrics before work is approved. Once a project is greenlit, teams create a practical plan, break work into shippable increments, estimate effort, and identify dependencies and risks. During execution, teams work in short cycles using a project board, pull requests, and regular reviews to maintain momentum and quality.

The approach relies on clearly defined roles and strong communication. Project Managers coordinate delivery, schedules, risks, and stakeholder communication; Product Managers define outcomes and priorities; Developers build and test the work; and QA/Testing validates acceptance criteria. Teams meet regularly through daily standups, weekly planning and delivery syncs, and milestone reviews, with a clear escalation path for blockers. This rhythm keeps work visible, helps surface risks early, and ensures stakeholders are informed as the project progresses.

Quality and risk management are treated as continuous responsibilities rather than final checkpoints. The team uses unit tests, integration tests, smoke tests, and CI security checks to reduce defects and validate that changes meet requirements. Risks are tracked in a Risk Register, monitored weekly, and escalated when they affect delivery or customer commitments. At the end of each sprint or release, retrospectives capture lessons learned and turn them into action items with owners and due dates so the team improves iteratively over time.

## Core Principles

- Customer-first: prioritize customer value and usability
- Iterative delivery: ship small, testable increments
- Clear ownership: every project has named leaders and accountable roles
- Data-informed decisions: measure impact and adapt based on evidence
- Psychological safety: encourage feedback, learning, and continuous improvement
